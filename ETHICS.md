# Ethics

## Representation

The story comes from the author's own taxi journey. All depicted characters use fictional appearances. No identifiable likeness, voice, or personal detail of a real driver or passenger is used without informed consent.

The driver is the main character. The film shows his skill, patience, and physical effort. It avoids exaggerated poverty, pity, and villainous passengers.

## Disclosure

The finished film and the radio cut end with a visible line: `AI-generated film. Characters and appearances are fictional.`

The narration is synthetic. Do not present it as the voice of a real person.

## Consent

If a real person is referenced during development, get consent before using their story, voice, or image. Consent for a story is not consent for a synthetic voice clone.

## Language and culture

Amharic narration is the primary audio. English subtitles are provided for wider access. Keep both faithful to each other. Do not add slang or claims that are not in the source story.

The setting is Addis Ababa. Avoid visual clichés that turn a working street into a tourist backdrop.

## Audience testing

Tell viewers the cut is AI-generated before they give feedback. Ask for honest reactions to story and pacing, not praise. Do not publish quotes or metrics without permission.

## Data and credentials

API keys live in `.env` and are never committed, printed, or logged. `runs.jsonl` records model parameters, not credentials.

Generated media and caches are gitignored. Treat narration, images, and video as project assets, not public releases, until reviewed.

## Limits of the pipeline

Automation can produce a coherent cut but cannot verify cultural accuracy, pronunciation, or whether the result treats the driver with dignity. A human review is required before release.
